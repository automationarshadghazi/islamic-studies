2. However, Muslims are held strictly accountable before God for solemn, deliberate oaths (*muʿaqqadah*) sworn intentionally, and are commanded to honour and keep their oaths.
3. If a Muslim breaks a deliberate oath, Islam prescribes expiation (*kaffarah*): feeding ten poor (*masakin*) at the average standard of one's family, clothing ten poor people, or freeing a slave; or if financially unable, fasting for three days.
4. Verse 90 strictly prohibits four corrupt practices — intoxicants (*khamr*), gambling (*maysir*), idolatrous stone altars (*ansab*), and divining arrows/superstition (*azlam*) — describing them as impure abominations from Satan's work (*rijs min ʿamal al-shaytan*).

6. Strictly shunning sins and honouring oaths leads believers to gratitude (*shukr*) and spiritual and worldly success/prosperity (*falah*).

### ✅ FULL-MARK `5/5` MODEL ANSWER (Notice: Zero copying, 6 distinct crisp points in 7 lines!):
> Qurʾan 5:89–90 teaches Muslims both accountability in speech and strict abstinence from corrupting sins. First, verse 89 highlights Allah’s mercy by stating that believers are not punished for accidental or thoughtless slips in their oaths (*laghw*). Second, however, Muslims are held strictly accountable for deliberate, solemn oaths and are commanded to honour their word faithfully. Third, if a deliberate oath is broken, a Muslim must offer expiation (*kaffarah*) by feeding or clothing ten poor people to the average standard of their own family, freeing a slave, or—if unable—fasting for three days. Fourth, verse 90 categorically forbids intoxicants (*khamr*), games of chance (*maysir*), sacrificing at stone altars (*ansab*), and superstition through divining arrows (*azlam*), marking the final stage of progressive revelation banning alcohol. Fifth, the passage identifies these practices as spiritual filth devised by Satan to corrupt human beings. Finally, avoiding such abominations and keeping promises enables believers to show gratitude to Allah and attain true prosperity (*falah*) in this world and the hereafter.

## Question `1(c)` — `[10 Marks]` *(Target time: 19 minutes | Structure: Define/Frame + 2 Agree Paras + 2 Counter-Argument Paras + Weighed Nuanced Conclusion)*

### Question Reminder:
*‘Avoiding forbidden (haram) actions is more important for a Muslim than performing good deeds.’ Discuss this statement with reference to different points of view. [10]*  
👉 **TRIGGER PHRASE TO ATTACK:** **"MORE IMPORTANT THAN"**!

### ✅ FULL-MARK `10/10` (LEVEL 5) MODEL ANSWER:
> In Islam, a believer’s obedience encompasses both abstaining from Divine prohibitions (*haram*)—such as *shirk*, murder, intoxicants, and falsehood—and actively fulfilling righteous deeds (*amal salih*), including the Five Pillars and voluntary charity. Whether avoiding *haram* is **more important than** doing good deeds is debated across classical scholarship and scripture.
>
> **Arguments Supporting the Statement (Why Avoiding *Haram* Can Be Prioritised):**  
> Strong textual evidence supports the view that shunning prohibitions takes precedence because major sins directly destroy the spiritual value of good deeds and harm wider society. In a famous Hadith in Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet (pbuh) explicitly contrasted the two: *"What I have forbidden for you, avoid it completely; and what I have commanded you, do of it as much as you are able."* Classical scholars explain that avoiding *haram* requires sheer restraint accessible to everyone rich or poor, whereas positive deeds depend on capacity. Furthermore, **Qurʾan 25:68–70** warns that invoking another god (*shirk*), murder, or adultery incurs doubled punishment, and **Qurʾan 4:116** establishes that unrepented *shirk* nullifies every good deed a person ever performed. Similarly, the Prophet’s Hadith of the "bankrupt person" (*al-muflis*) describes a worshipper arriving on Judgment Day mountains-high with prayer, fasting, and *zakat*, yet cast into Hellfire because he slandered, cheated, and harmed others—proving that unrestrained *haram* wipes out mountains of worship.
>
> **Counter-Arguments (Why Performing Good Deeds Is Equally or More Vital):**  
> Conversely, other Muslims argue that merely avoiding *haram* is passive and insufficient on its own to fulfil the purpose of creation, which is active worship (*ibadah*, **Qurʾan 51:56**). A person who never drinks alcohol, steals, or lies, yet completely abandons the obligatory Five Pillars—especially daily prayer (*salah*), which the Prophet (pbuh) called the dividing covenant of faith—fails their fundamental duty to God (**Hadith 73**). Moreover, scripture teaches that active good deeds actually erase minor sins rather than the reverse: **Qurʾan 11:114** states, *"Verily, good deeds remove evil deeds,"* and **Qurʾan 25:70** promises those who repent, believe, and **work righteous deeds** that Allah converts their past evils into good. Psychologically and spiritually, *taqwa* gained through fasting (**Qurʾan 2:183**) and prayer (**Qurʾan 29:45**, *"Prayer restrains from shameful and unjust deeds"*) provides the inner strength without which avoiding temptation is nearly impossible.
>
> **Evaluative Level 5 Conclusion (Weighing Strength + Nuanced Alternative Judgement):**  
> Overall, the statement is **convincing only with regard to grave sins (*shirk* and harming human rights, *huquq al-ʿibad*)**, because no quantity of voluntary good deeds can compensate for polytheism or unredeemed injustice against people. However, as a sweeping generalisation, claiming one is strictly *more important* than the other creates a false dichotomy: in Islam, obligatory worship (*fard*) and abstaining from *haram* are **two inseparable halves of *taqwa***. Alternatively, the relationship is best judged by category—avoiding *haram* outweighs **optional (*nafl*)** good deeds (for example, refusing interest-based income matters far more than giving voluntary *sadaqah* from unlawful wealth, since *"Allah is Pure and accepts only what is pure"*), whereas fulfilling **obligatory (*fard*)** pillars stands on at least equal footing with avoiding sin.

## Question `2(a)` — `[10 Marks]` *(Choice 1 — Practices / Section 1.7)*

### Official Mark Scheme Checklist for Level 4 (`9–10/10`):
- Define *Hajj* (5th Pillar, obligatory once in a lifetime for financially/physically able adults — *istitaʿah*, **Qurʾan 3:97**, 8th–12th *Dhul-Hijjah* in Mecca).
- Accurate chronological walkthrough of key rituals **explicitly linked to equality**:
  1. **Entering *Ihram* at the *Miqat***: Men strip away tailored silk/suits/crowns to wear two simple unstitched white sheets (*izar* and *rida'*), women wear modest plain dress -> **erases class, wealth, royal/poor status**. Reciting the shared ***Talbiyah*** (*"Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk..."*) in one voice.
  2. ***Tawaf* (Circumambulating the *Kaʿbah* 7 times)** & ***Saʿi* (Walking 7 times between *Safa* and *Marwah*)**: Million-strong crowd moving anti-clockwise shoulder-to-shoulder around one Qiblah; recalling Hajar (an enslaved African woman!) whose footsteps kings and scholars honour equally!
  3. **Stay at *Mina* & Standing on Plain of *ʿArafat* (*Wuquf* — 9th *Dhul-Hijjah*)**: Peak ritual (*"Hajj is ʿArafat"*), millions standing under the open sun begging Allah's mercy together, foreshadowing the Day of Resurrection where everyone stands bare and equal before Allah.
  4. **Night at *Muzdalifah***: Sleeping on the open ground under the stars collecting pebbles — no VIP palaces.
  5. **Stoning pillars (*Rami al-Jamarat*), Sacrifice (*Udhiyah / Nahr*), Shaving/Trimming (*Halq / Taqsir*) & *Tawaf al-Ifadah***: Shared rejection of Satan, meat shared with the poor globally, humility before Allah; reinforced by Prophet's **Farewell Sermon** (*"No Arab has superiority over a non-Arab, nor white over black, except by piety"*).

### ✅ FULL-MARK `10/10` MODEL ANSWER:
> Pilgrimage (*Hajj*), the fifth Pillar of Islam ordained in **Qurʾan 3:97** once in a lifetime upon adult Muslims possessing financial and physical capability (*istitaʿah*), is performed annually between the 8th and 12th of *Dhul-Hijjah* in and around Mecca. Every major ritual is deliberately structured to strip away worldly hierarchies and visibly embody human equality before Allah.
>
> First, at designated boundary stations (*miqat*), pilgrims make their intention (*niyyah*) and enter the sacred state of ***Ihram***. Men remove all stitched luxury clothing, uniforms, and status symbols to wear two identical, plain unstitched white cloths, while women wear simple modest attire without face covering. Rich billionaires and humble labourers look indistinguishable—resembling burial shrouds (*kafan*)—and chant the same ***Talbiyah*** (*"Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk — Here I am, O Allah, here I am"*), proclaiming equal servitude to one Lord.
>
> Second, upon entering Masjid al-Haram, pilgrims perform ***Tawaf***, circling the *Kaʿbah* seven times anti-clockwise side by side regardless of race or nationality, followed by ***Saʿi***, walking seven times between the hills of *Safa* and *Marwah*. Crucially, *Saʿi* commemorates Hajar—a black, formerly enslaved woman—whose trust in Allah every male and female pilgrim, ruler and citizen alike, must re-enact, powerfully overturning racial, class, and gender arrogance.
>
> Third, on the 8th pilgrims camp simply in tents at *Mina*, before gathering on the 9th of *Dhul-Hijjah* on the Plain of ***ʿArafat*** for *Wuquf* (*"Hajj is ʿArafat"*), standing together from noon to sunset in intense supplication (*duʾa*) and repentance. Standing beneath the same sky without privilege mirrors the Day of Judgment, where only *taqwa* distinguishes souls—the very spot where the Prophet (pbuh) delivered his Farewell Sermon declaring that no Arab is superior to a non-Arab, nor white to black, except through piety.
>
> Finally, at sunset pilgrims travel to ***Muzdalifah***, sleeping directly under the open sky on mats and collecting pebbles—eliminating material comfort gaps—before returning on the 10th to stone the pillars representing Satan (*Rami al-Jamarat*), offer the animal sacrifice (*Udhiyah/Nahr*) distributed in large part to the poor, shave or trim their hair (*Halq/Taqsir*) in shared humility, and complete *Tawaf al-Ifadah*, emerging spiritually renewed and equal as newborn infants.

## Question `2(b)` — `[15 Marks]` *(Choice 1 — AO2 Evaluation)*

### Question Reminder:
*‘Pilgrimage (Hajj) creates greater unity in the Muslim community (ummah) than any other act of worship.’ Evaluate this statement. [15]*  
👉 **TRIGGER PHRASE TO ATTACK:** **"GREATER UNITY ... THAN ANY OTHER ACT OF WORSHIP"**!

### ✅ FULL-MARK `15/15` (LEVEL 5) MODEL ANSWER:
> Unity of the worldwide Muslim community (*ummah*)—described by the Prophet (pbuh) as *"one body: if one limb aches, the whole body responds with fever and sleeplessness"*—is a central aim of Islamic worship (*ibadah*). Whether *Hajj* generates **greater unity than any other act of worship** requires weighing its unmatched global scale against its once-in-a-lifetime frequency and limited accessibility compared with daily prayer, Ramadan, and *zakat*.
>
> **Arguments Supporting the Statement (Strength of *Hajj* as the Supreme Global Unifier):**  
> Strong evidence supports *Hajj* as uniquely powerful because it is the **only physical gathering of the entire global *ummah*** in one geographical space. Over two million Sunni, Shiʾi, and Sufi Muslims from every continent, language, and ethnicity converge simultaneously in Mecca, transforming the abstract belief in one *ummah* into lived reality—famously inspiring figures like Malcolm X to abandon racial separatism after witnessing white, black, and Asian pilgrims eating and praying as brothers in *Ihram*. Unlike local mosque worship, which often reflects a single neighbourhood class or ethnicity, *Ihram*, *ʿArafat*, and sleeping outdoors at *Muzdalifah* physically erase wealth and national borders. Moreover, through **Eid ul-Adha** and the *Udhiyah* sacrifice synchronised with *Hajj*, even the 1.8 billion Muslims who cannot travel to Mecca unite simultaneously with the pilgrims in shared worship and meat distribution to the poor.
>
> **Counter-Arguments (Limitations of *Hajj* & Greater Practical Unity in Other Pillars):**  
> However, a critical weakness of claiming *Hajj* creates *greater* unity than any other worship is its **exclusivity and infrequency**. Under **Qurʾan 3:97**, *Hajj* is obligatory only once in a lifetime and only for those financially able (*istitaʿah*); strict national quotas, rising travel costs, ill health, and poverty mean the vast majority of Muslims worldwide **never physically attend *Hajj* once in their entire lives**. An act experienced once—or never—cannot sustain day-to-day neighborhood solidarity as consistently as regular worship.
>
> By contrast, **congregational prayer (*Salah* & *Jumuʿah*)** builds daily, lifelong grassroots unity five times a day and every Friday in every village on earth. Standing shoulder-to-shoulder and foot-to-foot behind one Imam, rich and poor neighbours check on the sick and bereaved every single day, while all Muslims globally face the exact same *Qiblah* synchronously. Equally, **fasting in Ramadan (*Sawm*)** unites nearly every adolescent and adult Muslim on earth simultaneously for thirty consecutive days every single year—rich and poor feeling identical hunger from dawn to sunset, breaking fast together at communal *iftars*, standing together in nightly *Tarawih*, and sharing wealth through ***Zakat*** and ***Zakat ul-Fitr***, which directly bridges economic divisions within society rather than merely masking wealth temporarily for five days under white cloth.
>
> **Evaluative Level 5 Conclusion (Weighed Judgement + Alternative Conclusions):**  
> To conclude, whether *Hajj* creates greater unity depends directly on **which dimension of unity is measured**. If unity is defined **symbolically and internationally** across racial, sectarian, and national lines, *Hajj* stands unmatched as the ultimate visible summit of the global *ummah*. However, if unity is measured by **practical, sustained, everyday social cohesion inclusive of poor Muslims**, *Hajj* alone is insufficient because millions never attend and class differences can re-emerge once pilgrims return home. Therefore, the strongest judgement is that **Ramadan and congregational *Salah* build deeper, continuous local unity across every class every year**, whereas ***Hajj* crowns that daily foundation as Islam’s supreme international expression of brotherhood**.

### Section B — Choice 1: Question 2 (Purification for Prayer & Mind — `1.5`)
- **2(a)** Explain the rituals of purification (*taharah*) required for prayer in Islam and their spiritual significance. **[10]**
  - **Mark Scheme [9–10/10]:** Link physical & spiritual purity (*"Purity is half of faith"* — Muslim; **Qurʾan 5:6** command to wash before prayer; **Qurʾan 2:222**). Detail: (1) Removing physical filth (*najasah*) from body, clothes, and clean prayer place; (2) **Minor ablution (*wudu*)** after minor impurities: *niyyah* + *Bismillah*, washing hands 3x, rinsing mouth & nose 3x, washing face 3x, forearms to elbows 3x, wiping head (*masah*) & ears, washing feet to ankles 3x — Hadith that sins wash away from eyes, hands, and feet with the last drop of water!; (3) **Major ablution (*ghusl*)** full-body wash after marital relations, menstruation (*hayd*), or postnatal bleeding (*nifas*); (4) **Dry ablution (*tayammum*)** with clean earth/dust when sick or lacking water (**Qurʾan 5:6** showing Islam's ease/mercy); (5) Purifying the mind from worldly distractions to achieve humble focus (*khushuʿ*).
- **2(b)** ‘Purifying the mind from worldly thoughts is much harder and more important than purifying the body.’ Evaluate this statement. **[15]**
  - **Mark Scheme [13–15/15]:** *Agree:* *Wudu* takes 2 minutes of water whereas *Shaytan* whispers constantly during prayer (*khanzab*); without pure *niyyah* (**Hadith 1**), *khushuʿ* (**Qurʾan 23:1–2** *"Successful indeed are the believers, those who humble themselves in their prayers"*), and *shahadah* cleansing *shirk*/envy/arrogance, physical washing is mere hygiene (**Qurʾan 107:4–6** heedless worshippers). *Disagree:* Legally, **prayer is completely invalid without *wudu/ghusl/tayammum*** (*"Allah does not accept prayer without purification"* — Muslim), whereas wandering thoughts diminish reward without invalidating the obligation; physical washing itself triggers mental readiness; **Qurʾan 5:6** commands outer washing directly. *Level 5 Conclusion:* Outer purification is the **indispensable legal key** that unlocks prayer's validity, whereas inner purification of mind and heart is **harder to master and determines the spiritual depth and reward** of worship—both working together harmoniously.

### Section B — Choice 2: Question 3 (Diversity of Devotion: *Ashura*, *Du'a* & Role of Family — `1.6`)
- **3(a)** Explain the diverse ways in which Muslims express devotion to God through Ashura and personal prayers (*duʾa*). **[10]**
  - **Mark Scheme [9–10/10]:** Highlight diversity within global Islam: (1) **Ashura (10th of Muharram)**: For **Sunni Muslims**, voluntary day of fasting (often paired with 9th or 11th Muharram) commemorating Allah saving Prophet Musa (pbuh) and the Israelites from Pharaoh — Prophet fasted it in Medina saying it expiates the previous year's minor sins, plus family generosity/charity; for **Shiʾi Muslims**, solemn day of grief and commemoration recalling the martyrdom of **Imam Husayn ibn ʿAli** (grandson of the Prophet) and his family standing against tyranny at **Karbala (680 CE)** — expressed through *majalis* sermons, elegies (*latmiyyah*), black attire, charity water/food stalls (*sabil*), and standing against injustice; (2) **Personal supplication (*duʾa*)**: Informal, any language, any time/posture (hands raised, during *sujud*, last third of night, *Salat al-Istikharah* for guidance, **Hadith 75**), contrasting formal Arabic *salah* (*"Duʾa is the essence of worship"* — Tirmidhi; **Qurʾan 40:60** *"Call upon Me; I will respond to you"*).
- **3(b)** ‘The family plays a greater role than the wider community in developing a Muslim’s faith.’ Evaluate this statement. **[15]**
  - **Mark Scheme [13–15/15]:** *Agree:* First school (*madrasah*) from birth — *Adhan/Iqamah* whispered in newborn's ear, *Aqiqah*, parents modelling daily *salah*, Ramadan pre-dawn *suhur*, moral manners (**Qurʾan 31:13–19** Luqman teaching his son *tawhid*, *salah*, humility; **Qurʾan 66:6** *"Save yourselves and your families from a Fire"*; Hadith every child born on *fitrah* and parents shape them); marriage (*nikah*) "completes half one's faith". *Disagree:* Toxic, non-practising, or convert families (like Ibrahim's idol-carving father Azar, or companions who converted against Meccan families, **Qurʾan 31:15**!) require the **wider *ummah*, mosques, *madrasahs*, Friday *Jumuʿah* sermons, Ramadan *Tarawih*, peer companionship, and *Hajj*** to sustain and educate faith, especially in secular modern societies. *Level 5 Conclusion:* In childhood, a pious family lays the deepest emotional foundation, whereas in adolescence, adulthood, or for converts/non-practising homes, the wider *ummah* and mosque community become decisive.
