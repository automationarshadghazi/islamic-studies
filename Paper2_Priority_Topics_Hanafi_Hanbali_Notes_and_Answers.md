# How to answer the questions
## Choose the whole question, not one easy part
For Section A, compare your knowledge of all three parts before choosing Question 1 or 2. Use the marks and command printed on the paper. [1]

**State / identify [5]:** five distinct, accurate items. A sixth relevant item can be a safety margin, not extra marks or a guarantee. Do not spend the time writing an essay. If asked for conditions, do not answer with unrelated benefits.

**Explain [10], mainly AO1:** identify the issue and school, describe the rule accurately, explain conditions/evidence, then show its practical effect. Aim for several developed points rather than ten unexplained labels. If asked for one school, make that school the main answer.

**Compare / discuss [10], mainly AO2:** organise by issue, not one detached essay about each school. Use:
**Define > similarities > paired differences > judged conclusion.**
For a statement, test the claim with arguments and counterarguments. Explain why an example supports or limits the claim.

## A paragraph that does useful work
“On [issue], Hanafi law holds X, whereas the standard Hanbali position is Y. [Named source] records the rule. This changes [practical outcome]. It supports/qualifies the claim because [reason].”

**Useful planning budget:** roughly 4 minutes for a 5-mark part, 18 minutes for AO1 [10], 20 for AO2 [10]. Adapt this within the 90-minute paper, allowing time to choose questions, answer Section B and check.

**Avoid:** invented quotes, unsupported percentages of agreement, blanket “stricter” labels and treating recommendations as validity conditions. These methods improve relevance; none guarantees a mark.

# 1. Divorce — learn deeply
**Evidence:** Qur’an 4:35 (arbitration), 2:229 (kind retention/release and compensation), 2:228 and 65:1–4 (‘iddah/procedure).

**Talaq:** husband’s divorce declaration or exercise of a valid delegated power. **Raj‘i:** revocable during ‘iddah under its conditions. **Ba’in:** not restored by simple unilateral return; minor/major irrevocability differ. Not every first divorce is automatically revocable.
**Khul‘:** compensated release, classically normally consensual. **Faskh:** dissolution on a recognised legal ground. **Tafwid:** delegated divorce power, not an automatic right to every requested separation.

## Three secure contrasts
**Coerced spoken talaq:** Hanafi generally legally effective; Hanbali qualifying coercion prevents effectiveness. Ordinary requests/pressure are not automatically legal duress. Mughniyya reports the positions. [5]
**Non-maintenance:** classical Hanafi inability alone does not ordinarily create the same dissolution remedy; enforcing support/authorised borrowing can matter. Hanbali permits seeking dissolution for inability to provide required support, with conditions. [6]
**Non-pregnant irrevocable divorcee:** Hanafi generally maintenance + housing; standard Hanbali generally neither. Separate pregnancy and revocable divorce, where provision duties apply. [7]

## Agreements and internal differences
Both mainstream classical schools generally count explicit triple talaq in one sitting as three; Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim defend one. That is their alternative, not “the Hanbali rule”. [24, 47]
Hanafi khul‘ normally counts as one irrevocable divorce. Hanbali can treat it as faskh with appropriate wording and without talaq intention; narrations differ. [25]
Both commonly understand the relevant three quru’ as menstrual periods. Pregnancy: until delivery. Do not turn every irregular cycle into a three-month rule. [5]

# Divorce: how to write it
## Practice AO1 [10]
**“Explain divorce according to one school of fiqh.”**
Choose Hanafi. Plan four developed areas: talaq and revocability; khul‘/delegation/court remedies; relevant ‘iddah and support; procedure versus legal effectiveness. Give evidence and qualifications. Do not merely list disagreements with other schools.

## Practice AO2 [10]
**“Hanafi and Hanbali divorce rules protect spouses in the same ways.” Discuss.**
A useful answer can develop this argument:

“Both schools regulate the ending of marriage through revelation, recognising talaq, compensated release and waiting periods. Qur’an 2:229 requires kind retention or release, so neither regards arbitrary harm as the purpose of divorce law.

However, access to a remedy differs. Classical Hanafi law does not ordinarily grant the same dissolution remedy solely because a husband cannot maintain his wife. Hanbali law permits the wife to seek dissolution for inability to provide required support, subject to conditions. The latter can provide an exit where the husband will not pronounce talaq.

Financial consequences point in a different direction. For the non-pregnant irrevocable divorcee, the Hanafi rule generally provides maintenance and housing, while the standard Hanbali position generally provides neither. This can make Hanafi protection stronger in that particular financial situation. Pregnancy and revocable divorce must be distinguished.

Therefore, the schools share objectives but not every mechanism. Protection depends on whether the issue is access to separation or financial provision; neither school is always more protective.” [6, 7]

**Why this works:** it tests the exact claim, pairs each comparison and reaches a qualified judgement. For a full answer, develop further relevant evidence or coercion/khul‘ detail; do not assume this extract earns ten marks.

# 2. Prayer: combining and times
**True jam‘:** the recognised prayer pairs in one prayer period. **Jam‘ suri:** first prayer at the end of its own time, second at the start of its own time. **Qasr:** shortening eligible four-rak‘ah prayers to two. Combining and shortening are different concessions.

**Pairs:** Zuhr + ‘Asr; Maghrib + ‘Isha. Fajr is not combined.
**Hanafi:** true combination normally restricted to the specified Hajj combinations at ‘Arafat and Muzdalifah, with conditions. Ordinary travel does not automatically permit it.
**Hanbali:** broader qualified travel, illness and recognised hardship concessions; weather rules have details. Not every journey or rainy moment qualifies. Dorar reports the comparison; [11] explains Hanafi apparent combination. [9, 11]

## Fajr, ‘Asr and Maghrib
**Fajr:** both true dawn to sunrise. Al-Quduri records the Hanafi preference for clearer dawn light (isfar); Ibn Qudamah’s Hanbali account generally prefers prayer at the beginning of its time, with specified exceptions. Preference is not a different valid window. [37, 38]

**‘Asr:** Abu Hanifa’s well-known rule is twice the object’s height **plus noon shadow**. Abu Yusuf and Muhammad hold once plus noon shadow, agreeing with the Hanbali rule here. Al-Quduri names both students. [17, 37, 38]
**Example:** 1 m object + 0.2 m noon shadow = **2.2 m versus 1.2 m**. Never turn this into a fixed 45–60-minute difference everywhere.

**Maghrib:** both begin at sunset. Do not invent a Hanafi–Hanbali difference from the old Sunni–Shi‘i comparison.

**Recall task:** define all three concessions; calculate the shadows; explain one practical difference and two similarities without looking.

# Wudu: know the actual pair
**Evidence:** Qur’an 5:6. Al-Quduri records Hanafi rules; al-Hajjawi’s Zad al-Mustaqni‘ records the standard Hanbali obligations quoted in [20]. [20, 37]

**Intention:** Hanafi Sunnah for wudu, not a validity condition; Hanbali required.
**Order:** Hanafi Sunnah; Hanbali obligatory.
**Continuity:** Hanafi recommended; Hanbali obligatory under its rules.
**Head:** Hanafi quarter minimum, whole Sunnah; Hanbali whole required.
**Mouth/nose/ears:** Hanafi Sunnah in wudu; standard Hanbali obligatory. Mouth/nose are obligatory in Hanafi ghusl, a different case.
**Feet:** both wash bare feet including ankles. One complete wash may fulfil the washing obligation; three is Sunnah completion.

## Nullifiers: high-value comparisons
**Ordinary contact:** Hanafi touch alone does not invalidate. Standard Hanbali relevant skin contact with desire invalidates; without desire it does not. Discharge has separate rules. [18, 37, 38]
**Direct genital contact:** Hanafi touch alone does not invalidate; Hanbali relevant direct hand contact without a barrier does in the standard account. [19, 36, 38]
**Camel meat:** Hanafi no invalidation; Hanbali invalidation. [26, 38]
**Blood/vomit:** Hanafi flowing wound blood and mouthful vomiting invalidate. Classical Hanbali large relevant impure discharge can also invalidate: not a blanket opposite rule. [26, 37, 38]

**Attribution trap:** Ibn Qudamah’s ‘Umdat al-Fiqh lists ear-wiping as Sunnah; reports from Ahmad differ. Do not attach al-Hajjawi’s obligation rule to every Hanbali authority. [38, 42, 48]

**Test:** someone washes without ritual intention, out of order, wiping a quarter-head. Explain the differing minimum-validity outcomes, not which performance is ideal.

# Worship: how to answer
## Practice AO1 [10]
**“Explain how two schools apply rules about combining prayers and prayer times.”**
1. Define jam‘, distinguish jam‘ suri/qasr, name the pairs.
2. Explain Hanafi Hajj restriction and apparent combination outside it.
3. Explain qualified Hanbali concessions; avoid a universal permission.
4. Compare ‘Asr with named early disagreement and a calculation.
5. Add shared Fajr/Maghrib rules and Fajr preferred-time distinction, if relevant.

## Practice AO2 [10]
**“Differences in worship rules divide Muslims.” Discuss with two schools.**
Define division: legal variation need not mean separate religions. Develop shared revelation/prayer obligations; paired practical differences; the challenge of coordinating congregations; and the possibility of respecting recognised disagreement.

## A worked comparison paragraph
“Both schools base purification on Qur’an 5:6. Nevertheless, al-Quduri records a quarter-head minimum in Hanafi law, whereas al-Hajjawi’s Hanbali account requires the whole head. Intention and order also have different legal status. Someone whose washing meets Hanafi minimums may therefore need additional actions under Hanbali rules. This is a significant practical difference, but it does not establish disagreement about the need for purification or the authority of the Qur’an.” [20, 37]

**Judgement:** “The consequences depend on how disagreement is handled. Different timings and validity rules can require coordination, but shared duties and acceptance of recognised jurisprudence need not produce religious division.”

**Avoid:** an AO2 answer that merely lists six wudu differences; a timing answer that spends most of its space on divorce; or an AO1 answer that argues without explaining the law.

# 3. Employment in local contexts
**Evidence:** Qur’an 4:29, unjust consumption; 83:1–3, dishonest dealing. The Bukhari report about withholding a worker’s wage supports fair payment.

## Learn six ethical principles
Clear lawful duties; an agreed wage; timely payment; honest fulfilment; no exploitation; accommodation of religious obligations. Identify whether the question asks for ethics, validity conditions or practical problems: these are not identical lists.

## Analyse tasks, not just employers
Both Hanafi and Hanbali law prohibit riba, fraud and prohibited activities. A job directly preparing an interest-bearing agreement raises a different question from work for a mixed business whose activities need further investigation.

Ask: What does the worker do? How directly does it assist the prohibited activity? Are there lawful duties or alternatives? What facts about need/necessity are established? Is a concession attributed to a named scholar with conditions?

**Local context:** compare access to lawful work, prayer facilities, contracts, Friday attendance, shift patterns and alternatives. A non-Muslim country does not automatically make prohibited work permissible; a Muslim-majority employer is not automatically lawful in every activity.

**Avoid the old stereotype:** “Hanafi permits overseas bank/supermarket jobs; Hanbali bans all indirect work.” No blanket school contrast follows from a job title, geography or ‘urf.

**Answer skeleton:** shared ethical principle > specific job duty > difficulty in that locality > possible lawful adjustment > qualified judgement.

**Practice prompt:** “Muslims should accept any available job if supporting a family.” Discuss. Consider the importance of earning/support, moral limits, actual necessity, alternatives and tailored advice. Do not turn hardship into an automatic exemption.

# Debt, riba and mortgages
**Evidence:** Qur’an 2:275, trade/riba distinction; 2:280, time or relief for a distressed debtor; 2:282, recording deferred obligations; 4:29, lawful mutual dealing.

**Riba:** an agreed increase for lending money over time is not the same contract as a genuine sale at a fixed deferred price. Gharar and gambling are separate prohibitions, not synonyms for interest.

**Mortgage analysis:** is the contract an interest-bearing loan, a genuine sale, a lease or a partnership? Who owns the asset, bears risk and receives each payment? A label such as “Islamic”, or a price benchmark, does not settle validity by itself.

**Possible lawful structures:** genuine murabaha purchase/resale; diminishing partnership with properly structured lease; benevolent interest-free qard hasan. Each needs its conditions; not every product using the name qualifies.

**Both schools prohibit riba.** Do not write “Hanafi permits conventional mortgages; Hanbali prohibits them.” Contemporary necessity/need concessions are disputed and conditional, not automatically either founder’s rule.

## A secure two-school trade comparison
**Khiyar al-majlis:** Hanafi offer/acceptance normally binds a valid sale unless another cancellation right applies. Standard Hanbali preserves a session option until separation or qualified termination/waiver.
**Named sources:** al-Kasani/al-Marghinani for Hanafi and Ibn Qudamah for Hanbali, cited in the comparative study [12].
**Example:** changing one’s mind before leaving can trigger a Hanbali session option; remaining in the shop alone is insufficient in Hanafi law. Defects or an agreed option are separate matters.

**Priority:** a mortgage/employment question needs contract and ethical analysis first, not an unrelated session-option essay.

# Finance: how to answer
## Practice AO1 [10]
**“Explain Islamic guidance concerning debt and mortgages.”**
Plan: prohibition/evidence; accurate distinction between loan interest and sale profit; debtor hardship/recording; genuine alternative structures and their conditions. Name your schools where relevant, but do not invent a disagreement where they agree.

## Practice AO2 [10]
**“Living in a non-Muslim country makes conventional borrowing acceptable.” Discuss.**
A useful line of argument is:

“Both Hanafi and Hanbali law prohibit riba, so geography by itself does not remove the prohibition. Qur’an 2:275 distinguishes permitted trade from riba. A genuine sale at a deferred price and a loan requiring interest must therefore be analysed as different contracts.

Nevertheless, Muslims may encounter limited housing finance and serious family needs. This raises a debate about necessity or need, lawful alternatives and the terms of particular contemporary opinions. Such circumstances deserve investigation rather than dismissal.

However, difficulty does not automatically establish a valid exception. The availability of renting or other finance, the severity of harm and the actual contract matter. Any concession must be attributed and its conditions stated; it cannot simply be described as ‘the Hanafi overseas rule’.

Thus local conditions affect the practical options and evaluation, but do not make every interest-bearing loan acceptable. A careful answer distinguishes a general prohibition from a specifically justified exception.”

**Why this works:** it weighs the statement, applies evidence and avoids a false school split. Add a checked named modern position only if you can state it accurately; do not manufacture one to decorate the essay.

# 4. Qur’an, Sunnah and Hadith
**Qur’an:** primary revelation; context, general/specific commands, progressive revelation and interpretation matter. Learn the Meccan/Medinan distinction for source questions. [1]
**Sunnah:** Prophetic teaching and practice, known through reports; explains/specifies Qur’anic commands. Example: the general duty of prayer and its Prophetic performance. Authority and application are related but not identical questions.

## Hadith assessment: five useful criteria
1. A connected chain of transmission.
2. Upright/trustworthy transmitters (‘adalah).
3. Precision in memory/transmission (dabt).
4. Absence of disqualifying irregularity (shudhudh).
5. Absence of a disqualifying hidden defect (‘illah).
Assess isnad and matn. Do not reduce authenticity to “it sounds reasonable”.

**Sahih:** meets the relevant authenticity requirements. **Hasan:** accepted with a lower precision level in the relevant classification. **Da‘if:** requirements not met; **mawdu‘:** fabricated. Weak is not synonymous with fabricated; grades and legal implications can be disputed.

## Your selected schools
Hanafi systematic reasoning does not mean rejecting Hadith. Ahmad is associated with preferring certain usable weak/mursal reports over unsupported opinion/analogy when no stronger contrary evidence exists. Early terminology does not match every later classification exactly. This does not authorise fabricated reports or every weak report. [21]

**Critical distinction:** a report’s authenticity does not alone settle every ruling. Jurists also consider meaning, scope, reconciliation and other evidence.

**Recall task:** name the five criteria, explain one possible defect, then distinguish authenticity assessment from legal interpretation.

# Hanafi and Hanbali methods
**Founders:** Abu Hanifa (d. 767), Kufa; Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 855), Baghdad. Abu Yusuf and Muhammad al-Shaybani helped develop Hanafi law and sometimes disagree with Abu Hanifa.

**Shared:** Qur’an/Sunnah as foundational, authoritative consensus, disciplined interpretation and recognised analogy. Do not write “reason versus revelation”.

**Qiyas:** extend a rule through a shared effective cause (‘illah). Example: explaining the extension of an intoxication prohibition to a new intoxicant. Show the cause, not simply “two things resemble each other”.
**Hanafi emphasis:** extensive systematic qiyas and istihsan: leaving an apparent analogy for stronger legal justification, not personal convenience.
**Hanbali emphasis:** transmitted evidence, including Companion precedents, before unsupported opinion/analogy. Hanbalis do not reject all qiyas. Ahmad is cautious about unsupported claims of consensus. [21]
**‘Urf:** lawful custom may clarify contractual terms in both schools; it cannot simply legalise a clear prohibition.

## Which opinion belongs to whom?
Al-Quduri documents Hanafi law. Al-Hajjawi and Ibn Qudamah are later Hanbali authors. A founder’s qawl, a riwayah from him, a later jurist’s selection and a relied-upon position are not identical. [20, 37, 38]
Mughniyya is the comparative reporter of many family/inheritance rules, not their Sunni-school originator.

**Syllabus limit:** the Imams “and their Madhab” are specified, not a first-generation-only rule. Later authorities can document a school; their individual alternatives must be labelled. Wider Unit 2.5 source vocabulary still matters even when your practical pair is Hanafi/Hanbali. [1]

**Do not infer** that Ibn Taymiyyah’s individual preference always equals standard Hanbali law.

# Sources: how to answer
## Practice AO1 [5]
**“Identify five criteria used in assessing an authentic Hadith.”**
Use the five criteria on the earlier page, separately and clearly. Do not substitute five book titles or five legal sources.

## Practice AO1 [10]
**“Explain how primary sources guide the derivation of law.”**
Explain Qur’an and Sunnah authority, textual context, Hadith assessment and interpretation. Develop an example such as prayer or purification. If the question says primary sources, do not spend the whole answer on qiyas/‘urf.

## Practice AO2 [10]
**“Hanafi and Hanbali methods are fundamentally different.” Discuss.**
Use this worked argument as a model:

“Both schools treat Qur’an and Sunnah as foundational, so it is misleading to contrast human reason with revelation. Each requires interpretation and accepts disciplined legal reasoning.

However, Hanafi jurisprudence is especially associated with systematic analogy and istihsan. Hanbali accounts give particular priority to transmitted evidence and Companion precedents before analogy. These differences of emphasis can influence legal outcomes.

For example, al-Quduri records different wudu minimums from the Hanbali obligation set in al-Hajjawi’s text. This gives a concrete consequence of interpretation, but does not show that either school denies Qur’an 5:6. Nor does every practical difference follow from one simple methodological rule.

Therefore, the schools share their primary foundation more than the statement suggests. Their weighting and interpretation of evidence create meaningful differences without making them unrelated systems.” [20, 21, 37]

**Improve it:** add a second well-understood example and a clear explanation; avoid claiming all members of a school agree on every detail.

# 5. Female inheritance
**Evidence:** Qur’an 4:7, 4:11–12 and 4:176. Always state the surviving heirs. Examples assume only the named relevant heirs and the net estate after prior claims.

## Shared starting points
**Wife:** 1/4 without qualifying descendants; 1/8 with them. Multiple wives share that entitlement.
**Daughter:** a sole daughter without a son has a 1/2 fixed share; two or more share 2/3 without a son; sons/daughters take residue at 2:1.
These are starting rules, not complete answers for every estate. Women do not always receive half a man’s share. [8]

## Two valid Hanafi–Hanbali contrasts
**A. Father and paternal grandmother only:** Hanafi father excludes his own mother (the deceased’s paternal grandmother). Standard Hanbali allows her 1/6, father taking the remainder. Mughniyya cites Ibn Qudamah’s al-Mughni for this exception. [8]

**B. Paternal grandfather and full sister only:** Abu Hanifa gives the grandfather everything and excludes the sister. Standard Hanbali gives grandfather 2/3, sister 1/3. Abu Yusuf and Muhammad also give 2/3 plus 1/3 here: do not claim Hanafi unanimity. Mughniyya reports the disagreement. [8]

## Do not reuse the old contrast
A sole daughter with a qualifying paternal uncle can receive 1/2 while the uncle takes the residue in both schools. The old Sunni–Ja‘fari example is not a Hanafi–Hanbali difference.

**AO2:** link outcomes to specific relationships and interpretation. Financial responsibilities may explain part of the framework, but do not assume every man supports every female relative or every woman ends up wealthier.

**Recall task:** explain the grandmother’s precise relationship; calculate both scenarios without changing the list of heirs.

# Inheritance: how to answer
## Worked estates of 120,000 units
**Case A: father + his mother only.**
Hanafi: grandmother 0; father 120,000.
Standard Hanbali: grandmother 20,000 (1/6); father 100,000.
**What to explain:** the father blocks his own mother in Hanafi law, while the Hanbali exception permits her share alongside him. [8]

**Case B: paternal grandfather + one full sister only.**
Abu Hanifa: grandfather 120,000; sister 0.
Standard Hanbali: grandfather 80,000 (2/3); sister 40,000 (1/3).
Abu Yusuf and Muhammad: the same 80,000/40,000 result in this particular case. [8]

## Practice AO1 [10]
**“Explain female inheritance with reference to two schools.”**
Plan: Qur’anic basis; shared wife/daughter rules with qualifications; grandmother case; grandfather/sister case and named internal disagreement. Use fractions and relationships, not just “women inherit half”.

## Practice AO2 [10]
**“The two schools differ greatly over women’s inheritance.” Discuss.**
Define the extent of difference. Explain shared revealed shares first, then show where blocking/residue rules change a woman’s actual entitlement. Evaluate significance: the same core framework can produce a large difference for a particular heir.

**Possible judgement:** “Differences are limited in their shared fixed-share foundations but can be decisive for a particular woman. The grandmother or sister may receive a share under one position and nothing under another; one cannot judge the extent from the 2:1 slogan alone.”

**Check:** fraction totals, which grandmother, which grandfather/sister, whether other heirs exist, and whether you have confused Abu Hanifa with every Hanafi jurist.
